# Nagarro — Tone of Voice

Canonical voice rules for the Copywriter agent. Applies across **all channels** (deck copy, internal/external emails, LinkedIn posts, event notifications, webpage copy). Channel-specific rules are at the bottom.

> **Source:** Distilled from the `anthropic-skills:nagarro-linkedin-posts` skill, which is itself grounded in real Nagarro Product Studio LinkedIn posts and reviewed examples. Last updated 2026-05-20.

> **Scope note:** The base voice was defined by **Nagarro Product Studio**. For other Nagarro contexts (parent-brand financial updates, internal HR comms, etc.), use the channel adjustments at the bottom — the core voice does not change.

---

## 1. The voice in one line

We sound like **a thoughtful, experienced colleague** writing to a smart, busy reader — not like a brand account.

Human, not robotic. Personal, not distant. Professional, not formal. Confident, not promotional.

---

## 2. The 3 Cs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Clear** | Communicates expertise. The reader knows what we mean on the first pass. |
| **Concise** | Respects the reader's time. Every word earns its place. |
| **Consistent** | Builds trust. Same terms, same rhythm, every time. |

---

## 3. Voice traits

| Trait | What it means | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Confident** | We know our craft | "We match you with the right designer in 24 hours." | "We try to help companies find talent." |
| **Human** | Partners, not a platform | "We stay close. Regular check-ins, no surprises." | "Our proprietary system optimizes designer allocation." |
| **Specific** | Concrete beats vague | "Start in 7 days vs. the 66-day industry average." | "We help you hire faster." |
| **Warm** | We care about the outcome | "No commitments, just possibilities." | "Fill out the form to begin the procurement process." |
| **Expert** | We know design and product | Reference real practices, tools, frameworks | Overly casual; never dumb it down |

---

## 4. Always do

- **Lead with the most important idea.** Cut introductory filler ("In today's competitive landscape…" → delete entirely).
- **Make "you" the hero, not "we".** Rewrite "we" → "you" wherever the sentence still works. *Exception:* MoU/partnership/investor announcements where "we" / "Nagarro" is appropriate.
- **Stay active. Stay present.** Active voice. Present tense for things already true.
- **Ground every claim in a number, client name, or outcome.** No floating adjectives.
- **Vary sentence length deliberately** — short for impact, medium for rhythm. Never three short choppy sentences in a row.
- **Combine related ideas into one stronger sentence** — use commas, colons, subordinate clauses.
- **Use plain business English.** Pick the simpler word every time.
- **One CTA per piece**, near the end, phrased as a natural next thought.
- **Read it aloud.** If you stumble, rewrite.

---

## 5. Never do

- **No superlatives without proof.** "World-class", "best-in-class", "cutting-edge", "top-tier" — banned unless followed by hard evidence in the same sentence.
- **No urgency manipulation.** "Don't miss out", "Last chance", "Limited time" — banned.
- **No vague transformation language.** "Transform your business", "drive growth", "unlock potential" — banned.
- **No buzzword stacks.** "Cutting-edge, next-generation, AI-powered" — banned.
- **No consulting jargon.** See vocabulary swap list (§7).
- **No AI-cliché contrast openers.** "X is growing. Y isn't." / "Most companies do A. Few do B." / "Everyone talks about X. Nobody does Y." / "The problem isn't X. It's Y." — all banned.
- **No repetitive openings.** Three "We" / "Our" / "Nagarro" sentences in a row = rewrite.
- **No crafted slogan endings.** If the closing line sounds like it belongs on a poster, rewrite it.
- **No em dashes.** Use commas, colons, or en dash only when essential.
- **No semicolons.**
- **No all-caps** except logos/brand names.
- **No emojis** in deck copy, emails, or formal posts. (LinkedIn posts: see channel rules.)
- **No italic text** in LinkedIn posts.
- **No Lorem ipsum / dummy copy.** Ever. (Designer/QA also enforce this.)

---

## 6. Sentence structure rules

1. Lead with the most important idea.
2. Mix short, medium, and longer sentences. Don't optimize every sentence to the same length.
3. One idea per paragraph in posts; up to two related ideas in emails/decks.
4. Combine related thoughts with commas, colons, or subordinate clauses — don't split into staccato.
5. Use transitions between paragraphs: "That's why…", "Which means…", "The result:", "Here's what that looks like in practice."
6. Preserve human imperfection — not every sentence needs to be perfectly balanced. Real writing varies.

**Before/after — choppy → coherent:**

> Bad: "Our team is global. We work fast. We deliver results. We understand your needs."
>
> Good: "Our team is distributed across 39 countries, which means we move at the speed your product needs, not the speed of a single time zone."

**Before/after — split → combined:**

> Bad: "We understand your goals. We match the right designer. They start in 7 days."
>
> Good: "Once we understand your goals, we match the right designer and have them ready to start within 7 days."

## 7. Vocabulary — preferred swaps

| Instead of | Use |
|---|---|
| sophistication | complexity |
| security posture | security approach |
| capabilities | tools / skills |
| visualisation | reporting |
| cost implications | costs |
| holds up under scrutiny | works in practice |
| leverage | use |
| enable | help |
| empower | help |
| unlock value | create value |
| unlock potential | (rewrite — name the specific outcome) |
| end-to-end capabilities | full coverage |
| robust framework | clear approach |
| transformation journey | change process |
| strategic imperative | priority |
| best-in-class | strong / proven |
| world-class | (cut, unless paired with proof) |
| cutting-edge | (cut, unless paired with proof) |
| engagement model utilisation | how we work together |
| design talent solutions | designers ready to join your team |
| scalable resourcing | scale up or down as you need |
| stakeholder alignment | everyone on the same page |

### Banned phrases (zero tolerance)

leverage · enable · empower · strategic imperative · transformation journey · unlock value · unlock potential · best-in-class / world-class (without proof) · robust framework · end-to-end capabilities · drive transformation · increase strategic agility · unlock operational value · seamless · synergy · disrupt (as a verb)

### Abstract noun budget

These words are acceptable, but **no more than once or twice per piece**, and every use must be grounded in a concrete outcome (faster delivery, lower costs, better decisions, stronger security, easier scaling, reduced complexity):

capabilities · posture · flexibility · architecture · workflows · transformation · ecosystem · compliance

> Bad: "We help organisations build scalable cloud architecture that enables digital transformation across their ecosystem."
>
> Good: "We help teams ship faster and spend less on infrastructure as the business grows."

## 8. Punctuation conventions

- **Em dash:** never. Use a comma, colon, or rephrase.
- **En dash:** only when truly essential (ranges, paired ideas) — and only in long-form (deck copy, webpage). Avoid in posts.
- **Semicolons:** never.
- **Contractions:** always (we're, you'll, it's). Posts and emails sound robotic without them.
- **Quotation marks:** straight `"` in code/files; smart `"…"` in body copy.
- **Lists:** use sparingly. Coherent paragraphs beat bullet stacks.
- **Numerals:** numerals for stats (1000+, 24 hours, 7 days, 70%, 400+). Spell out in narrative ("dozens of clients", "hundreds of designers").

---

## 9. Opening patterns

A good opener creates **tension or a specific observation** the reader recognizes — it does not announce.

**Templates that work:**

- Specific named problem: *"Most cloud migrations don't fail on the technical side. They stall because the architecture wasn't built to keep up with how the business actually moves."*
- Direct expert claim: *"AI agents change what testing means."*
- Concrete framing: *"Often, AI stops at insight instead of driving action."*
- Hard number: *"Sixty-six days is how long it takes to hire a UX designer. Your next sprint starts Monday."*

**Banned opener structures (read as AI immediately):**

- "X is growing. Y isn't."
- "Most companies do A. Few do B."
- "Everyone talks about X. Nobody does Y."
- "The problem isn't X. It's Y."

---

## 10. Closing patterns

A good close lands the main idea or leads naturally into the CTA. It does not sound rhetorically engineered.

**Templates that work:**

- Plain forward-looking: *"Getting the infrastructure right early saves a lot of time and money later."*
- Invitation that fits: *"If that's the kind of problem you're sitting with, it's worth a conversation."*
- Belonging close: *"1,000+ companies didn't wait 66 days. Neither should you."*

**Avoid:**

- Poster-style slogans: *"Data should drive your next move, not just document your last one."* — rewrite as a plain sentence.
- Generic CTAs: *"Contact us to learn more."* — replace with something specific.
- Feature claims as endings — always end with a thought or a CTA, never a feature.

---

## 11. Quality bar — before you ship

Every piece must pass these checks (Copywriter applies; Quality Manager re-verifies):

**Voice and tone**
- [ ] Sounds like a thoughtful colleague, not a brand account
- [ ] No banned phrases (§7) or AI-cliché openers (§9)
- [ ] No superlatives without proof, no urgency manipulation
- [ ] Every abstract concept grounded in a concrete outcome
- [ ] Closing line is natural, not a slogan

**Human writing**
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary
- [ ] Related ideas combined where possible
- [ ] No three consecutive "We"/"Our"/"Nagarro" openers
- [ ] Transitions connect paragraphs

**Mechanics**
- [ ] No em dashes
- [ ] No semicolons
- [ ] Contractions used
- [ ] No Lorem ipsum / no `<TODO>` / no placeholder strings
- [ ] One CTA only (where a CTA applies)

**Reads aloud** without stumbling.

## 14. Channel adjustments

The base voice (§1–§13) does not change. These are the channel-specific deltas.

### 14a. Deck copy (.pptx)

- **Headlines:** ≤ 8 words. Equip Extended.
- **Body copy:** 1–3 short sentences per text block; no walls of text. Equip.
- **Bullet lists:** acceptable for index slides, principle grids, and comparison tables. Never on hero or chapter-divider slides.
- **Tense:** present for capability statements; past for case studies.
- **Stat tiles:** numeral + 1–2 word label. No sentences inside a tile.
- **Footer values:** use Brief defaults (see `templates/powerpoint/rules.md §4b`).
- **No emojis. No exclamation marks. No em dashes.**

### 14b. Internal email

- Conversational, second-person ("you", "us as a team").
- Subject line: ≤ 8 words, lead with the verb or the news.
- Open with the ask or the news in the first 1–2 lines.
- Length: ≤ 200 words unless explicitly requested longer.
- Close with a clear next action and an owner.
- Contractions on. No emojis unless the Brief notes a casual context.

### 14c. External email

- Same as internal, with two adjustments:
  - Slightly more formal opener (no "Hey team")
  - Always sign off with name + role + Nagarro (or Nagarro Product Studio if applicable)
- Never use first names for the recipient unless the Brief confirms an existing relationship.

### 14d. LinkedIn post

- Follow post anatomy: **Hook → Body → CTA → Tags**.
- Hook: 1–2 lines that create tension or land a specific observation.
- Body: 2–5 short paragraphs. Use blank lines generously — LinkedIn compresses text.
- One CTA, one link, near the end.
- Hashtags: **never auto-add.** Suggest 3 options, max 3 per post; default include `#FluidicIntelligence` for parent-brand posts.
- Emojis: 1–2 max, only for culture/product posts; **none** for partnerships, investor updates, or thought-leadership posts.
- Tagging: ask the user before adding @-mentions. Never invent names.
- See `anthropic-skills:nagarro-linkedin-posts` skill for full post templates by type.

### 14e. Event notification (internal or external)

- Lead with: what · when · where · who it's for. In that order.
- Time format: `1:00 pm CEST` (lowercase am/pm, timezone explicit).
- Date format: `May 15, 2026` for external; `15 May` for internal where year is implied.
- One CTA: Register here / Add to calendar / Join the call.

### 14f. Webpage copy

- Headlines hold up scanned. Sub-headlines complete the thought.
- Sections lead with the benefit, not the feature.
- No copy that only makes sense in sequence — every section must stand alone for skimmers.
- CTAs match the page's single primary intent.

## 15. Before/after — full rewrites

### Generic AI corp → Nagarro voice

> Bad: "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape, organizations must leverage cutting-edge AI capabilities to unlock value and drive transformation across their ecosystem. Our world-class team is uniquely positioned to empower your team with end-to-end solutions."
>
> Good: "AI works best when it changes how a team decides, not just what they see in a dashboard. We've built that change into product engagements at Star Alliance, BMW Group, and Moody's, and we can plug into yours in seven days."

### Choppy → flowing

> Bad: "Our designers are skilled. They work fast. They join your team quickly. We support them all the way."
>
> Good: "Our designers ship fast, slot into your team within a week, and stay supported by the studio behind them."

### Slogan close → plain close

> Bad: "Data should drive your next move, not just document your last one."
>
> Good: "Data is most valuable when it helps teams act faster."

### Vague claim → grounded claim

> Bad: "We help organizations transform faster."
>
> Good: "Teams start with us in 7 days. Industry average is 66."
