## Brand anchor

Read before writing:
- `brand/tone-of-voice.md` — voice rules (internal comms section applies)
- `brand/positioning.md` — Nagarro's values and culture signals

### Internal comms principles
- **People-first language** — put the impact on people before the business rationale
- **Lead with the "what"** — state the news in the first paragraph; context follows
- **Acknowledge the human dimension** — especially for org changes or sensitive news
- **No corporate jargon** — no "synergies", "restructuring exercise", "going forward"
- **Be specific** — vague reassurances land badly; concrete facts build trust
- **Sign off as a person** — name and role, not "The Management Team"

## Step 1 — Collect the brief

Ask using `AskUserQuestion`. You need:

1. **Communication type** — all-hands, leadership change, policy update, org change, recognition, town hall follow-up, or other?
2. **The news** — what exactly is being announced or communicated?
3. **Audience** — whole company, a specific team, a region, or leadership only?
4. **Sender** — who is signing this? (name + title)
5. **Tone** — celebratory, matter-of-fact, empathetic, or a mix?
6. **Key points to include** — 3–5 things that must be in the piece
7. **Anything sensitive** — anything to handle carefully or avoid?
8. **Format** — email body, letter (with header), or plain text?

---

## Step 2 — Write the draft

### Structure for most comms
```
[Subject line if email — direct, not teasing]

[Opening — state the news in 1–2 sentences]

[Context — why this is happening; 1–2 short paragraphs]

[Impact on people — what this means for the reader specifically]

[Next steps — what happens next, and when]

[Close — human, warm, signed by name]
```

### Sensitive comms (leadership change, restructuring, incident)
- Lead with empathy, not process
- Acknowledge uncertainty if it exists — don't paper over it
- Give a clear "what happens next" even if details are still being worked out
- Avoid passive voice: "we have decided" not "a decision has been made"

---

## Step 3 — Self-review

Before returning the draft, check:
- First paragraph states the news clearly — no burying the lede
- No corporate jargon or buzzwords (see `brand/tone-of-voice.md`)
- Every statement is specific — no vague reassurances
- Tone matches the situation (celebratory news ≠ org change tone)
- Signed off by a named person

---

## Step 4 — Return the draft

Return the finished communication ready to send, plus a one-line note on any sensitive
handling decisions made (e.g. "Led with empathy before business rationale given the restructuring context").
