# Executive Summary Slide

## Overview
High-level overview slide summarising the key points of the proposal or presentation. All variants are light by default.

## Light / Dark Mode
All variants support light and dark mode. Mode can be switched per slide via variables.

## Variants

### Executive Summary - 3 Col, Title as First Col / Light
- Layout uses 3 columns
- The first column is the slide title
- The remaining two columns hold the summary content
- Columns are vertically centered

### Executive Summary - 3 Col, Title Above / Light
- Layout uses 3 columns for content
- Title sits above the three columns
- Columns are vertically centered

### Executive Summary - 2 Col Wide, Bottom Left / Light
- Content occupies the space of 2 out of 3 columns (wider text area)
- Text flows continuously (not split into two columns)
- Everything aligned to the bottom left

### Executive Summary - 2 Col Wide, Title Top + Text Bottom / Light
- Same wide 2-column content area as the above variant
- Title aligned to the top left
- Body text aligned to the bottom left

## When to Use
- Use when the deck needs to give a busy reader the key takeaways in 60 seconds
- Position: 4th slide in the deck, after Cover → Index → Confidentiality Statement
- Always comes before "A Note From Us" — Executive Summary first, personal note second
- Triggered by brief content such as: "key points", "summary", "TL;DR", "highlights", "at a glance"
- Especially important in proposals and pitches where the decision-maker may not read every slide
- Choose variant based on copy length:
  - **3 Col, Title as First Col**: use for medium-length summaries (3–6 bullet points) — the title column creates breathing room
  - **3 Col, Title Above**: use for longer summaries that need all 3 columns for content
  - **2 Col Wide, Bottom Left**: use for short, punchy summaries (2–4 lines) where white space adds impact
  - **2 Col Wide, Title Top + Text Bottom**: use when the title is a strong statement and the body copy is a brief follow-through

## Usage Rules
- Use early in the deck, typically right after the cover or index
- Keep copy concise — this is a summary, not a detailed breakdown
- For longer summaries, prefer the 3-column variants to keep the slide balanced
